# Workit — First-Users Playbook (30 days)

_Goal: go from ~12 test accounts to a small, **liquid** pocket of real activity —
enough that a real employer posts a job and gets real offers fast. Liquidity in
one small area beats emptiness across Gauteng._

---

## 1. Pick the wedge (do this first)

- **One area.** Your own suburb + the 2–3 suburbs next to it. Somewhere you can
  physically show up and where you already know people. Density matters more than
  size — workers and jobs must be close enough to actually match.
- **2–3 categories**, high-frequency + easy to fulfil:
  - **Gardening / lawn** · **House cleaning** · **Moving & errands (bakkie/lifts)**
  - These need little equipment, happen constantly, and a result is obvious — good
    for early trust + ratings.
- Write it down: _"Workit is live for gardening, cleaning and errands in
  [suburb] + neighbours."_ Everything below targets exactly that.

## 2. The sequence (don't skip the order)

1. **Supply first — recruit 15–25 verified workers** in the wedge. A stocked,
   verified bench is what makes the first employer's experience good.
2. **Manufacture first demand — you post 5–10 real jobs** (your own household/
   business tasks, family, friends, neighbours). Pay real money. Now workers see
   activity, not a ghost town, and you get the first ratings on the board.
3. **Recruit employers** into proven liquidity: _"post a job, get verified workers
   nearby in an hour."_ That's a real promise now, not a hope.
4. **Let the loops run** — referrals (built in), repeat hires, ratings.

## 3. Week-by-week

**Week 1 — Supply.** Hit the worker channels (§4). Target: 15+ workers signed up,
10+ through verification. You personally walk the first few through it (WhatsApp
video call) so the funnel doesn't leak. Approve them promptly (you're the gate).

**Week 2 — Seed demand.** Post your own 5–10 jobs across the 3 categories. Hire,
pay, rate. Ask each worker to rate you back. Screenshot good outcomes — that's
your marketing. Fix anything that felt clunky (tell me).

**Week 3 — Employers.** Post in local community groups (§4), pitch 1:1 (§5). Aim
for 5–10 real employer jobs from people who aren't you. Be the concierge —
personally make sure each gets offers fast.

**Week 4 — Loops + learn.** Push referrals to everyone active. Review the metrics
(§6). Decide: tighten the wedge, or expand to the next suburb? Repeat what worked.

## 4. Where to find them (Gauteng, ground game)

**Workers:**
- Neighbourhood + township **WhatsApp groups**; "jobs in [area]" groups.
- **Facebook groups** for your suburbs (community / buy-sell / jobs).
- **Physical**: hardware stores (Builders/local), garden centres, taxi ranks,
  spaza shops, church/community noticeboards, the local labour-corner where day
  workers already gather — a flyer there is gold.
- **Word of mouth**: every worker you sign refers 2 more (it's built in).

**Employers:**
- Suburb **estate / complex / ratepayer WhatsApp & FB groups** (where people
  already ask "anyone know a good gardener?").
- Your own network first — friends, family, colleagues with homes.
- Property managers, complex caretakers, small businesses needing odd jobs.

## 6. Metrics that actually matter (watch these, not vanity signups)

- **Liquidity (north star):** % of posted jobs that get ≥1 offer within ~1 hour.
  If this is high, the marketplace works. If low, recruit more supply in the wedge.
- **Verified worker count in the wedge** (your usable bench).
- **Time-to-first-offer** on a new job.
- **First repeat hire** (someone hires a second time = product-market pull).
- **Completed + rated jobs** (trust compounding).
- Jarvis can read most of these live — _"how many jobs got an offer this week?"_

## 7. Founder hustle (the unsexy truth)
Marketplaces are started by hand, not by ads. For the first ~50 jobs **you are
the algorithm** — personally recruiting workers, sourcing jobs, making sure every
early job succeeds. It doesn't scale, and that's fine; it's how every marketplace
got its first liquidity. Automate only once it's working manually.
