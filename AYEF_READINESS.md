## 2. Why this is our best fit yet

1. **Grant, not debt or equity** — no repayment, no dilution. Best possible terms.
2. **Milestone-linked tranches** — our funding proposal is *already* structured
   this way (3 tranches gated on verifiable platform data). We can hand them a
   milestone schedule most applicants can't produce.
3. **"Next generation of job creators"** is their framing — Workit doesn't just
   employ people, it *creates earning opportunities for thousands of others*.
   That is a stronger job-creation story than almost any applicant will have.
4. **🔑 MARKET ACCESS IS THE REAL PRIZE.** Absa banks tens of thousands of SA
   small businesses — **those are exactly our missing demand side.** The grant
   solves cash; an introduction to Absa's SME client base solves the actual
   business problem. **Ask for this explicitly in the application and in every
   conversation.** If we get one line into Absa Business Banking's customer
   communications, our employer problem is solved.
5. **Governance + financial-management support** — we genuinely need this
   (sole founder, no CFO, compliance items outstanding). Say so; coachability
   scores well in milestone-based programmes.

## 5. Answer bank — drafted responses to standard questions

_Copy-paste and trim to the form's character limits. Numbers current as at 2026-07-18._

### Q: What does your business do? (elevator, ~50 words)
> Workit is a South African digital marketplace that connects households and
> businesses to nearby, ID-verified workers for everyday jobs — gardening,
> cleaning, trades, deliveries, errands and care. It is free for workers, built
> for entry-level Android phones and low data, and available in English,
> Afrikaans and isiZulu.

### Q: What problem are you solving?
> Millions of young South Africans are capable of work but unemployable on paper:
> no matric, no CV, no references, no contactable work history. Meanwhile the
> everyday work that does exist — domestic, garden and small-trade jobs — is
> arranged by word of mouth, paid in cash, and leaves no record. A young person
> can work for three years and still be unable to prove a single day of it to a
> landlord, lender or employer. Workit removes the paper barrier to entry and
> creates the record that has never existed.

### Q: Who are your customers?
> Two sides. **Supply:** unemployed and underemployed South Africans aged 18–35
> seeking flexible income — currently 72 registered workers, 58% aged 18–34.
> **Demand:** households, small businesses, property managers, body corporates
> and estate agents in Gauteng needing everyday work done reliably and safely.

### Q: How do you make money? (business model)
> Free during launch, by choice — liquidity first. From Month 8, a light
> facilitation fee on confirmed jobs, plus optional paid features already built:
> featured/boosted job listings and a verified-business tier. Conservative
> projection: R36 500 in Year 1, scaling with job volume. Because the platform is
> built and the founder is technical, the marginal cost of each additional job is
> near zero.

### Q: What traction do you have?
> The platform is fully built, live and compliant. As at 18 July 2026: **90
> registered accounts (72 workers, 18 employers), 23 workers ID-verified, 58% of
> workers aged 18–34** — all acquired at effectively zero cost, with no paid
> advertising. Signups ran at 10–14/day during our first outreach push. The full
> marketplace loop (post → offer → hire → complete → rate → confirm payment) has
> been exercised end to end. We are pre-revenue and job volume is still minimal —
> generating employer demand is precisely what this funding addresses.

### Q: Who are your competitors and why will you win?
> SweepSouth (domestic cleaning, employer-priced) and informal WhatsApp groups
> and street-corner hiring. We differ in three ways: **(1) breadth** — all
> everyday work categories, not one vertical; **(2) worker agency** — workers set
> and counter-offer their own prices rather than accepting a platform rate;
> **(3) proof of work** — our Work Record turns informal jobs into a verifiable
> income and work history a worker can show a landlord or lender, which no
> competitor offers. Our safety architecture (SOS with GPS, doorstep start codes,
> trusted-contact sharing) is also built for South African conditions
> specifically.

### Q: Tell us about your team.
> **Tshepo M — Founder.** Designed and built the entire platform himself,
> including identity verification, fraud detection, the safety system and POPIA
> compliance infrastructure — without outside capital. Leads product,
> engineering, partnerships and employer acquisition.
> **Simphiwe Msibi — Operations Administrator (part-time).** Account approvals,
> verification review, worker support and disputes.
> **Planned with funding:** field ambassadors for on-the-ground employer
> acquisition and community worker onboarding.

### Q: What will you use the funding for?
> Employer demand generation, primarily. The platform is built and worker supply
> is proven; what we lack is capital to reach the businesses who can hire.
> Breakdown: field-based employer acquisition and community activation (~26%);
> founder and operations stipends (~42%); infrastructure and tools (~12%);
> one-off legal, compliance and equipment (~12%); operations and contingency
> (~8%).

### Q: What are your 12-month milestones? (⭐ AYEF is milestone-based — answer well)
> **Q1 (M1–3):** compliance complete (attorney sign-off + Information Officer
> registration); 150 active employers; 250 youth onboarded; 400 jobs completed.
> **Q2 (M4–6):** 400 employers; 600 youth onboarded; 300 earning; 1 100 jobs.
> **Q3 (M7–9):** 900 employers; 1 150 youth onboarded; 650 earning; 2 400 jobs;
> revenue model activated.
> **Q4 (M10–12):** 1 600 employers; 2 000 youth onboarded; 1 200 earning; 4 200
> jobs completed; **R1.68m paid directly to workers**.
> Every figure is recorded automatically by the platform and can be reported
> monthly, with a live administrative view available to the funder.

### Q: What is your social impact / how many jobs will you create?
> At Year-1 targets, 1 200 young South Africans will earn income through Workit,
> completing 4 200 jobs worth **R1.68 million paid directly to workers**. Against
> a R250 000 grant that is roughly **R208 per young person moved into paid work,
> and R6.70 into a young person's pocket for every R1 granted**. Beyond income,
> each worker accumulates a verifiable Work Record — proof of earnings and work
> history usable for housing, credit and formal employment applications.

### Q: What support do you need beyond funding? (⭐ ask for market access)
> Three things, in order of value to us: **(1) Market access** — introductions to
> Absa's small-business and commercial banking clients, who are exactly the
> employers our platform needs. This is the single most valuable form of support
> Absa could provide and would directly solve our primary constraint.
> **(2) Governance and financial management support** — we are a founder-led
> business without a finance function, and would benefit from structured
> guidance. **(3) Mentorship** on scaling a two-sided marketplace and on
> enterprise sales to SMEs.

### Q: What are the risks?
> Our primary risk is employer demand: without jobs, workers churn and impact
> targets fail. We mitigate by weighting spend to field-based acquisition, pacing
> worker intake to demand, and reporting monthly so shortfalls surface early —
> the milestone-linked tranche structure means a funder can stop if we miss.
> Secondary risks: key-person dependency on the founder (mitigated by a second
> administrator and documentation), worker data costs (low-data app, pursuing
> zero-rating), and safety incidents (two-sided verification, SOS with GPS, start
> codes, live location, disputes and reporting).
